## SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM

### Section label
*Why Fred exists*

### Headline
**PMs are translators. And the translation tax is killing your speed.**

### Body
Every feature you ship crosses six teams before it reaches a customer. Research becomes requirements. Requirements become tickets. Decisions get made in Slack and never captured. Specs drift the moment they're written. And by launch day, Marketing is still waiting for the brief, QA is reverse-engineering test cases, and Support hasn't been told what changed.

That's not a process problem. It's a context problem. Fred fixes it.

### Supporting stat (if available, validate before publishing)
*"60% of the average PM's week is writing, re-explaining, and chasing alignment — across teams that never quite have the full picture."*

### Visual direction
Three-column "before Fred" illustration:
- Left: A PM at the center of chaos — arrows pointing in every direction (Jira, Figma, Slack, Confluence, email)
- Right: Fred at the center — one input flowing cleanly to every destination
- No text labels needed; the contrast speaks for itself

## SECTION 3 — How it works

### Section label
*How Fred works*

### Section headline
**Fred runs alongside your entire workflow. From first signal to shipped feature.**

### Section subhead
Most AI tools drop you into a blank box. Fred starts from context — your research, your tickets, your designs, your decisions — and carries it all the way through.

---

### Phase 01 — Discovery
**Label:** Discovery

**Headline:** Discovery starts here.

**Body:**
Drop in interviews, support tickets, NPS, analytics data, churn reports, or raw notes. Fred reads across all of it, surfaces recurring themes, ranks insights by impact, and drafts an opportunity brief — so when you open the spec, you already know what you're solving.

**Fred's output:** Insight summary · Theme clusters · Opportunity brief

**Visual direction:** A stack of messy inputs (interview notes, a spreadsheet, a Slack export) → Fred processes → a clean, ranked insight report emerges. Tone should feel like "signal out of noise."

---

### Phase 02 — Spec Writing
**Label:** Spec

**Headline:** Your Live Spec, built in seconds.

**Body:**
Paste a Jira link or drop in your Figma screens. Fred reads the ticket, comments, and linked designs — then drafts acceptance criteria, edge cases, and open questions. You make every call. Fred handles the heavy lifting.

**Fred's output:** Live Spec · Acceptance criteria · Edge cases · Open questions

**Visual direction:** Reuse existing Jira + Figma + spec drafting videos. These already work — keep them.

---

### Phase 03 — Engineering Alignment
**Label:** Engineer

**Headline:** Engineers never wait. Neither do you.

**Body:**
Engineers ask questions directly in the ticket — Fred answers instantly from the spec. Your Live Spec is available inside Cursor too, so engineers have everything they need without leaving their editor. Gaps caught before a line of code is written.

**Fred's output:** Instant Q&A in Jira · Spec inside Cursor · Gap detection

**Visual direction:** Reuse existing engineer Q&A and Cursor integration videos.

---

### Phase 04 — Cross-Functional Distribution
**Label:** Distribute

**Headline:** Write once. Fred speaks every team's language.

**Body:**
Fred takes your Live Spec and generates what every team needs — a KB article for Support, test scenarios for QA, a GTM brief for Marketing, and release notes for customers. Each adapted for its audience, all from one source of truth.

**Fred's output:** KB article · QA brief · Marketing brief · Release notes

**Visual direction:**
A single "Live Spec" box at the center. Four arrows radiate outward — each pointing to a different team with their tailored output. This is the key visual for the whole page. Consider making it a hover/reveal interaction where each output expands to show a preview of what Fred generates.

## SECTION 4 — CROSS-FUNCTIONAL OUTPUTS (Detail)

### Section label
*What Fred generates*

### Section headline
**One spec. Four audiences. Zero re-writing.**

### Section subhead
Fred knows who needs what — and how to say it to each of them.

---

### Output Card 1 — Knowledge Base
**Icon:** 📚
**Title:** Knowledge Base
**For:** Support · Customer Success · Internal docs

**Headline:** "What changed, and why" — written for the people who talk to your customers.

**Body:** Fred reads the spec and generates a structured KB article — what the feature does, why it was built, common edge cases and how to handle them. Support teams have what they need before the first ticket lands.

**Example output preview:**
> *"Feature: Smart Notifications — What's new and how to explain it to customers. Smart Notifications now [does X]. This was built because [Y]. Here's what to tell customers who ask about [Z]."*

---

### Output Card 2 — QA Notes
**Icon:** 🧪
**Title:** QA Notes
**For:** QA engineers · Test leads

**Headline:** Test scenarios from the spec — no reverse-engineering required.

**Body:** Fred generates test cases directly from the acceptance criteria and edge cases in the Live Spec. Each scenario includes the expected behavior, the edge case, and any regression risk flagged during spec review. QA hits the ground running.

**Example output preview:**
> *"Test case: Empty state — 0 items in cart. Expected: Display [X message]. Edge case: User arrives via direct link with expired session. Regression: Check against [related feature]."*

---

### Output Card 3 — Marketing Brief
**Icon:** 📣
**Title:** Marketing Brief
**For:** Product marketing · Growth · Comms

**Headline:** The "what it does and why it matters" — not the implementation details.

**Body:** Fred translates your spec into a crisp GTM summary: what the feature does, who it's for, the customer pain it solves, and the positioning angle. Marketing gets the right story without a 45-minute handoff call.

**Example output preview:**
> *"Feature brief: [Feature X]. What it does: [one line]. Who it's for: [customer segment]. Why now: [business context]. Suggested positioning: [angle]. Key message: [one sentence]."*

---

### Output Card 4 — Release Notes
**Icon:** 🚀
**Title:** Release Notes
**For:** End users · Changelog · Product blog

**Headline:** What shipped, in plain English — in your product's voice.

**Body:** Fred writes changelog-ready release notes from the spec — accurate to the actual implementation, written for customers rather than engineers. No more "misc bug fixes and performance improvements."

**Example output preview:**
> *"You can now [do X in Y context]. We built this because [customer pain — in plain language]. [Optional: what's coming next]."*

## SECTION 5 — LIVE SPEC (Updated framing)

### Section label
*Why Live Spec?*

### Headline
**Your PRD is already out of date. Meet Live Spec.**

### Subhead (updated)
The spec that evolves with your product — and powers every team downstream.

### Body (updated)
The moment it's written, it starts drifting from reality. Engineers ask questions it doesn't answer. Decisions get made in Slack, never captured. By sprint end, nobody trusts it. A Live Spec fixes that — it evolves with the ticket, stays inside the conversation, and Fred keeps it current. And when it's done, it doesn't just sit in Notion. It becomes the source every team pulls from.

### Comparison table (updated)

| Old PRD | Live Spec |
|---|---|
| Outdated the moment it's saved | Evolves with every decision and conversation |
| Gaps found by engineers mid-sprint | Gaps caught before sprint starts |
| PM becomes the bottleneck | Fred answers engineering questions 24/7 |
| One spec for one audience | Automatically generates outputs for every team |
| Lives in Notion, visited once | Powers KB, QA, marketing, and release notes |
